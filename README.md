# Rock Finder

Tap a map to see the mapped geology, rock types, and minerals/crystals commonly found there, with real specimen photos.

**Hunt mode:** zoom in, drop a pin, and pick a search radius (0.5–20 miles). Rock Finder samples the geologic map across the circle and ranks the best places to hunt. It looks at the gems and minerals each spot's rocks hold, gem-friendly rocks like pegmatite and basalt, edges where rock types meet, and nearby streams, old mines and exposed rock. It also flags spots inside parks or tribal land where collecting isn't allowed.

The analysis also weighs topography (valley floors, ridges, steep slopes and flat basins from elevation data), mapped faults, dikes and veins, volcanic rock, volcanoes and hot springs, and mines and prospects documented by the USGS. For gravel and wash spots, it traces which hard or heavy stones wash down into the sediment from bedrock uphill. Each spot comes with a field guide for recognizing washes, old mine workings, faults, pegmatite and buried gravel layers, plus links to satellite view and old USGS topo maps. You can switch the map between Streets, Topo and Satellite.

Data: Macrostrat (CC-BY 4.0) for geology, faults and dikes; streams, mines, volcanoes and park boundaries from OpenStreetMap (Overpass API); documented mines and prospects from the USGS Mineral Resources Data System; elevation from the Copernicus DEM via Open-Meteo; base maps from OpenStreetMap, OpenTopoMap and Esri. Photos: Wikipedia / Wikimedia Commons.
